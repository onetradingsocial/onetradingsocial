On what makes a journal worth keeping

Trading journal questions, answered

What should I record in a trading journal?

At minimum: the setup, entry and exit prices, position size, and risk expressed in R. A useful journal goes further — a screenshot of the chart, the rationale behind the trade, your emotional state going in, and a mistake tag if something went wrong. Those last four are what turn a log into feedback.

How often should I review my trades?

Weekly is the practical minimum — often enough to catch a pattern before it costs you a month of results, infrequent enough to have a real sample. TradingSocial auto-generates a weekly review (Trader plan and above) covering P&L, win rate, drawdown, best setup and your costliest mistake.

Is there a free trading journal?

Yes. TradingSocial's free plan logs trades, setup tags and mistake tags with no card required, and keeps your last 30 trades visible. Nothing is ever deleted — upgrading to Trader adds unlimited history plus the weekly review.

What's the difference between a trading journal and a trade log?

A trade log is a record of what happened — entry, exit, size, result. A trading journal adds why: the setup, the rationale, how you felt, and what went wrong. That extra layer is what makes patterns visible and reviews possible.

Does a trading journal need to include losing trades?

Yes — a journal that only records winners can't tell you anything useful. Logging every trade, including losses and mistakes, is what makes the weekly review and personalised insights meaningful instead of a highlight reel.
