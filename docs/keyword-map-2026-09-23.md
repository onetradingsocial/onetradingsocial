# Keyword map — 23 September 2026

One target query per URL. There is no volume data, so difficulty is argued from
re-checkable evidence: who holds page one, and how many autocomplete variants a
stem produces (Google's public suggest endpoint). **No volume numbers are
invented below.**

Three standing facts shape every call:

1. **The differentiator has no search demand.** `verified track record`,
   `public trading profile` and `trading journal that proves` return between zero
   and six autocomplete variants, and those point at an unrelated product. What
   makes TradingSocial different has to be sold *on* pages won with other
   queries, never as the target of one.
2. **The category head terms are template queries.** Autocomplete for
   `forex/crypto/futures trading journal` is dominated by *template, excel, free,
   pdf, notion*. The software intent sits in the modifiers, not the stem.
3. **Peer SaaS holds page one, not media.** `prop firm trading journal` and
   `tradezella alternative` are almost entirely small vendor pages (TradesViz,
   Plancana, TradeLog, JournalPlus) — contestable in a way a review-site SERP is
   not, but contested by dozens of older domains.

## 2. Cannibalisation

Ranked by how directly the two pages collide.

1. **`/for/prop-firm` vs `/compare/best-prop-firm-trading-journal`** — both open
   with the *same sentence* as their first H2 ("Most challenges aren't lost to a
   bad strategy"), with near-identical FAQs. → `/compare/*` owns *choosing a
   journal*; `/for/prop-firm` owns *using ours during an evaluation*. Rewrite the
   `/for/prop-firm` H2 to a rule-set framing.
2. **`/for/mt5` vs `/compare/best-mt5-trading-journal` vs
   `/blog/what-an-mt5-statement-contains`** — all three explain statement import
   versus sync. → `/compare/*` owns the *choice*, `/for/mt5` owns *mt5 trading
   journal sync* (product), the post owns *what the file contains*. Cut the
   sync-vs-import explainer from the post to a two-line summary that links out.
3. **"What to record", three ways** — `/blog/what-to-record-in-every-journal-entry`
   (whole post), `/blog/why-a-trading-journal` (H2 "What a useful entry actually
   contains") and `/for/journal` (H3 "Everything worth recording"). → the new post
   owns it; the older post's section shrinks to a link; `/for/journal` keeps the
   feature framing but loses "everything worth recording" as a heading.
4. **Expectancy, three ways** — `/tools/expectancy-calculator` (the tool),
   `/blog/how-to-calculate-trading-expectancy` (the method) and
   `/blog/win-rate-is-lying-to-you` (H2 "Expectancy: The One Number That Tells You
   If Your System Works"). → tool owns `calculator`, new post owns `formula`, the
   old post must narrow to `win rate vs R-multiple` and drop the expectancy H2 to
   a link.
5. **Weekly review** — `/blog/the-twenty-minute-weekly-review` vs
   `/blog/why-a-trading-journal` (H2 "The weekly zoom-out") and `/for/journal`
   ("A weekly review, done for you"). → the new post owns the query; the old H2
   becomes a link.
6. **Backtesting** — `backtesting-vs-forward-testing` vs the new backtest post's
   H2 "What forward testing fixes, and what it does not". → shrink that H2 to a
   link; the older post owns `backtesting vs forward testing`.
7. **Drawdown** — the two drawdown posts publish 17 days apart but answer
   different questions (rule definitions vs statistics), and each says which in
   its first four words. No change; monitor only.
8. **Overtrading** — `patience-as-an-edge` and `the-psychology-of-revenge-trading`
   both argue discipline-after-emotion. Mild: let patience own `overtrading`,
   revenge own `trading after a loss`.
9. **`/` vs `/for/journal`** — both currently read as "trading journal". Resolved
   by the home-page retitle below. `/tools/position-size-calculator` vs
   `/blog/position-sizing-101` is fine as it stands; keep the post off the word
   "calculator".

demand, but the product does not do it and `/compare/best-mt5-trading-journal`
already says so.

## 4. Rewrites where the copy is off-target

Every claim below already appears on the page it is proposed for. Nothing
asserts tamper-proofing, an unfakeable record, or a lock over manually logged
trades — the three things `app/tests/unit/copy-claims.test.ts` pins.

**`/` — the biggest single miss.** "Social trading journal" reads as
copy-trading, which is the opposite of the product.

- Title (52): `TradingSocial — Trading Journal with a Public Record`
- Desc (144): `Journal every trade, get a weekly review, and build a public profile where each trade is labelled with its source. Free plan, or 14 days of Pro.`

**`/for/journal`** — currently aimed at the unwinnable head term.

- Title (52): `Trading Journal with a Weekly Review — TradingSocial`
- Desc (139): `An online trading journal that answers back: log each trade, get a weekly review, and read insights that state the sample size behind them.`

**`/for/forex`** — "Pairs & Sessions" is our framing, not a query.

- Title (51): `Forex Trading Journal with MT5 Sync — TradingSocial`
- Desc (140): `Log pips and R multiples per pair, split results across the London, New York, Asia and Sydney sessions, and import or sync your MT5 history.`

**`/for/futures`** — "for ES and NQ" targets nothing; the micros are the opening.

- Title (50): `Futures Trading Journal for Micros — TradingSocial`
- Desc (139): `A futures journal that knows contract size and tick value: MES, MNQ, ES, NQ, CL and GC logged in R multiples and reviewed a week at a time.`

**`/for/educators`** — targets a phrase nobody searches (fact 1).

- Title (52): `Trading Educator Platform with Proof — TradingSocial`
- Desc (145): `Build a public profile your audience can check: trades carry their source label, manual entries stay marked manual, and followers see the record.`

**`/tools/position-size-calculator`** — head term is a two-year play; the asset
variants are winnable now.

- Title (59): `Position Size Calculator for Forex and Gold | TradingSocial`
- Desc (145): `Free position size calculator. Enter account size, risk percentage, entry and stop for forex, gold, indices or futures and get the size to trade.`

**`/for` (hub)** — de-target it so it stops competing with `/for/journal`.

- Title (50): `Trading Journals by Market and Job | TradingSocial`
- Desc (135): `Pick the page that matches how you trade: forex, crypto, futures, MT5, prop-firm evaluations, or a public profile if you teach trading.`

**Leave alone:** `/pricing`, `/blog`, all `/compare/*` (their titles already match
their queries), `/tools/expectancy-calculator` (best-matched page on the site),
the legal pages and every existing blog title.

"people also ask" capture (search tooling returns links, not SERP features —
question-form autocomplete was used as the proxy). Built from the repo's markup
plus public SERPs and autocomplete: this says what each page *should* aim at,
not what it currently earns.
