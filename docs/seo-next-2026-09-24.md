## 1. The thing that is actually missing: a measurement loop

Fifteen PRs shipped on reasoning. Not one of them has been checked against a
search engine, because until 23 September there was nothing to check against.

From roughly **1 October** Search Console will hold a week of impressions,
average position and query data per page. That converts the next round from
guessing into arithmetic, and the rule is simple:

- **Position 11–20 for a real query** — the cheap win. The page already ranks;
  it needs depth, a better title, or a few internal links, not a rewrite.
- **Impressions but position 30+** — the query is too competitive for our
  authority today. Park it; it is a links problem, not a page problem.
- **Indexed, zero impressions** — the page is aimed at a query nobody types.
  The keyword map was a hypothesis; this is where it gets marked wrong.
- **Not indexed at all** — a technical problem, and the only category that
  needs urgent work. Check coverage reporting for the reason given.

**Nothing below this line should be prioritised until that data exists.** A week
of waiting is cheaper than a month of building the wrong page.

## 3. Buildable now, in the order I'd build them

### 3a. `/compare/tradervue-alternative`

The only major trading journal without a comparison page. You have TradeZella,
TraderSync, Edgewonk, Myfxbook and the spreadsheet. Tradervue is among the
oldest and most-searched names in the category, so this is a known-demand query
rather than a hypothesis — the one content gap I would fill without waiting for
data.

### 3b. Contextual internal links from the ten older blog posts

The sixteen posts carry 38 in-body links to landing pages, but the ten original
posts have **exactly two each**, which is the signature of a template rather
than of an editor choosing the most relevant destination. The six newer posts
range from one to five.

Footer links are boilerplate and search engines treat them as such; a link from
inside a paragraph about position sizing to the position-size calculator is
worth considerably more. This is an hour of work with a real effect on how
authority flows to the pages meant to convert.

### 3c. Structured data on the calculators and comparison pages

`FAQPage` on the comparison pages and `HowTo` or `SoftwareApplication` on the
calculators. This does not improve ranking directly; it changes how the result
*looks*, which moves click-through. Worth doing once the pages are known to have
impressions — which is another reason to wait for §1.

## 5. Things I would not do

Worth writing down, because each one is a standard suggestion that would cost
more than it returns here.

- **More landing pages on spec.** Eighteen is already more surface than a
  domain this age can support. Thin pages that earn nothing drag on how the
  whole domain is rated — the same reasoning that took the app sitemap from 38
  URLs to 6 in #70.
- **Buying links, or any directory's paid tier.** Already declined; the
  free listing carries the link, which is the part that matters.
- **Chasing "verified track record" as a keyword.** It is the product's
  differentiator and it has almost no search demand. Comparison and
  alternative queries are where that differentiator gets to do work, which is
  why the `/compare/` cluster exists.
- **Rewriting anything before §1 says it is broken.** Every page currently has
  a distinct target and no measured failure.
