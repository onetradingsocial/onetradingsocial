11 Found & fixed

Stripe only sends us 4 kinds of event, and the charge-related ones aren't among them

Stripe tells our app what happened by sending events to a webhook. The live webhook only has 4 event types turned on. Three that the trial depends on are missing:

customer.subscription.trial_will_end — the pre-charge email never sends
invoice.paid / invoice.payment_succeeded — we never record that a customer has paid
invoice.payment_failed — no failed-payment email

The middle one matters most because of your grace decision in item 02. Grace only applies to a customer who has paid us at least once, and that event is how we find out they paid. Without it, every customer looks like they have never paid. So a long-standing subscriber whose renewal fails loses access the same day — exactly the customer grace was kept for.

The first one breaks something else. The pre-charge email tells a trialist the amount, the date and how to cancel, three days before we charge them. The terms promise it. Right now it cannot send.

Fixed 17 September A dashboard setting, not a build: those four event types were added to the live webhook endpoint, which now sends 8. The code to handle each one was already deployed. No customer had reached day 11 or a renewal, so nobody was affected. The first trials reach day 11 on 27 September.

A related question, now closed: on our own test signup, the cancellation showed in Stripe but not in our database. The event did arrive. Stripe marked the cancellation with a cancel_at date and left the flag we read set to false, so our copy looked unchanged and was never updated. Fixed in 1a8473e, merged 17 September. The nightly job then corrected the record at 13:37 UTC the same day.

12 Found & fixed · merged

Checkout could restart the free trial, and bill a customer twice

Checkout only refused a customer who was currently on a trial. Three things got past that:

  • Cancel, let the trial lapse, and start another free trial. The terms say one per person.
  • Claim free referral months, cancel, and claim them again. Nothing recorded that they had been used.

Fixed 16 September Checkout now checks the customer's full history, in our records and in Stripe's. If it can't reach Stripe to check, it refuses rather than letting the purchase through. Referral months are recorded once used. One narrow gap remains: two checkouts opened at the same moment, before either finishes.

01 Decided · built

The referral reward paid out on a signup, not on a trade

Your call, 15 September The reward is earned by activation — the referred person logging a trade — and a payout can now only ever come through it. Someone who subscribes without trading earns their referrer nothing until they do. Built and tested. The referrals table is empty in production, so there is no historical payout to unwind.

A referral is supposed to earn its referrer a free month of Pro once the person they referred actually logs a trade. The payout is currently triggered by a subscription going live — and once every signup opens a trialing subscription, that fires the instant someone enters a card.

So a referred user can sign up, add a card, never place a single trade, and their referrer immediately earns a month of Pro. The "logged a trade" gate is skipped entirely.

One free month of Pro is A$50. The cap is six per referrer, so the worst case is A$300 of free service per person who shares a link widely enough. It costs us nothing today because nobody is trialing on Stripe yet — it starts the day this launches.

Still worth knowing: this restores the promise the referrals page already makes, but it does not change what a determined person could farm. Six throwaway accounts and six trades is still six months of Pro. The control that would actually close that is email confirmation, which is a Supabase setting currently switched off — the code for it is already in place and waiting.

02 Decided · built

A card that declines on day 14 bought 28 days of Pro

Your call, 15 September Grace drops to 7 days, and only for a subscription that has actually paid us at least once. A card that declines at the end of a trial now gets none at all. Built, tested and live in production. Since 17 September Stripe sends the payment events this depends on (item 11), so a paying customer now qualifies for grace, and the failed-payment email goes out when a card declines. You confirmed both on 18 September; no change was needed.

When a renewal payment fails, we give the customer 14 days of grace before their features switch off. That is deliberate and generous: their card expired, the bank held it, and Stripe is still retrying. They have done nothing wrong.

The trouble is that a failed first charge — the one at the end of a trial — looks identical to Stripe. So someone whose card declines on day 14 gets 14 trial days plus 14 grace days: 28 days of Pro without ever paying us anything, and an email confirming they keep their features. It is repeatable with a fresh account.

A$50 of service per occurrence. The code already holds the right principle — it refuses grace to a customer who has never paid — but the status Stripe uses for a failed trial conversion slips past that check.

Worth knowing what this trades away: a genuine new customer whose bank declines a first charge from an unfamiliar merchant — which is common — now loses access immediately rather than keeping it while they sort it out. The day-11 warning email and the payment-failed email both still go out, so they are told twice, but the features stop the same day. (Both emails depend on the Stripe events in item 11, so neither sends until that is fixed.)

03 Found & fixed

Changing plan during the trial would have charged people twice

This one was not on the list. It turned up while checking whether the billing portal lets a trialist switch plans (item 08), and it was created by this change rather than found in the old system.

Our checkout always creates a subscription — it has no way to modify one. That was fine when a trial was just a date on an account and buying a plan was the only way to get a subscription at all. It stopped being fine when the trial itself became a subscription.

The sequence: sign up, so a Pro trial is running and due to charge A$50 on day 14. On day 2, click the countdown in the nav and buy Trader at A$30. Nothing cancels the first one. The customer now pays A$80 a month having deliberately chosen one plan — and the one they chose is the cheaper of the two.
Fixed, no decision needed Checkout now refuses to start anything while a trial is running, and the in-app upsell sends a trialist to their billing page instead of offering a purchase. Nobody wants to double-bill, so this did not need asking.

This left a gap, which item 08 closed. With the in-app route shut, the billing portal is the only way for a trialist to switch to Trader. Plan switching is now on there, so they can.

04 Decided · published

What happens when someone is charged and meant to cancel

Your call, 18 September Refunds stay case by case, and the policy says that forgetting to cancel is the customer's responsibility. Section 11 of the terms now says exactly that. It points to the self-serve cancel and the three-day warning email. It also says what we do instead: cancel straight away, keep access to the end of the paid period, and refund only at our discretion. The Australian Consumer Law wording is unchanged. Live on tradingsocial.io/terms, dated 18 September 2026. This spells out the existing "no refunds for a change of mind" rule, so subscribers needed no 30 days' notice.

Today the terms say we do not refund a change of mind, that Australian Consumer Law rights are untouched, and that you should email us and we will work it out. That was written for people who chose to subscribe.

After this change the most common refund request becomes "I forgot to cancel" — from someone who never thought of themselves as a customer. We warn them three days ahead by email, and some will still miss it.

A refused refund that becomes a card chargeback costs the A$50 plus a dispute fee, and chargebacks count against the Stripe account. Refunding is almost always cheaper than winning.
  • Option AKeep it case-by-case and unpublished. Maximum flexibility, but the person reading the terms sees only "no refunds".
  • Option BPublish a short goodwill window — refund the first charge if they ask within, say, 7 days and have not used the paid month.
  • Option CStrict no-refunds beyond the legal minimum.
Recommendation Option B. At this volume the cost is negligible, it converts an angry chargeback into a cancelled subscription, and it is a genuinely good line to have on the pricing page while asking people for a card for the first time. I have deliberately left the terms unchanged pending your call.

08 Was a launch blocker · fixed

A trialist cannot switch from Pro to Trader — plan switching is off

Fixed before launch Plan switching is on in the live billing portal. Two related settings were changed at the same time. "End trials on subscription updates" is off, so switching plans during the trial doesn't end the trial and charge straight away. Promotion codes stay off in the portal.

The whole decision — everyone trials Pro and picks their real plan later — rests on the billing portal letting them switch. It does not. The portal configuration allows cancelling and updating a card, and nothing else:

subscription_update: false  ·  subscription_cancel: true  ·  payment_method_update: true
Default configuration, unmodified. Stripe ships this off; it has to be turned on and told which plans to offer.

So as things stand, the trial is Pro at A$50 or cancel. A customer who wanted the A$30 plan has nowhere to go — and item 03 closed the in-app route because it charged for both plans at once.

Recommendation Option A, before launch (done). It is a dashboard setting rather than a build, and without it two other things on this page have nowhere to happen — item 05's annual offer and the "then picks" half of the plan decision. Worth setting proration to "none" at the same time, so a switch during a trial does not raise a surprise charge.

10 Two-step fix

Stripe's own terms checkbox is switched off, and cannot be switched on yet

Stripe can require customers to tick acceptance of our terms on its checkout page and record it against the payment — a stronger record than anything we can store ourselves, because the acceptance and the transaction are the same object.

terms_of_service_url: NOT SET in the Stripe business profile, and the switch that turns the checkbox on is off in our own configuration. Both halves are needed, in that order — Stripe rejects the request if the URL is missing, so flipping our switch alone would break every checkout.

It has been a nice-to-have while checkout was rare. It is about to sit in front of every single signup, which is exactly when it stops being optional.

Recommendation Set the terms URL in the Stripe dashboard first, confirm the checkbox appears, then turn our switch on. Two small steps, and the order matters.
