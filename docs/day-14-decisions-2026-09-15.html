Day 14 Decisions

The 14-day trial is moving onto Stripe: a card at signup, and an automatic charge on day 14 unless the customer cancels. The engineering is built and sitting on a branch. Ten things are not engineering decisions — three were money leaking and are now closed, four are still yours to call, and three were Stripe dashboard settings I have since read off the API. One of those three is a launch blocker.

Money that was leaking

All three would have started costing real money on launch day, and all three are now closed. They are kept here in full rather than deleted, because the reasoning is what makes a decision reviewable later — and because in each case the fix does not cover everything, which is the part worth reading.

01 Decided · built

The referral reward paid out on a signup, not on a trade

Your call, 15 September The reward is earned by activation — the referred person logging a trade — and a payout can now only ever come through it. Someone who subscribes without trading earns their referrer nothing until they do. Built and tested. The referrals table is empty in production, so there is no historical payout to unwind.

A referral is supposed to earn its referrer a free month of Pro once the person they referred actually logs a trade. The payout is currently triggered by a subscription going live — and once every signup opens a trialing subscription, that fires the instant someone enters a card.

So a referred user can sign up, add a card, never place a single trade, and their referrer immediately earns a month of Pro. The "logged a trade" gate is skipped entirely.

One free month of Pro is A$50. The cap is six per referrer, so the worst case is A$300 of free service per person who shares a link widely enough. It costs us nothing today because nobody is trialing on Stripe yet — it starts the day this launches.

Still worth knowing: this restores the promise the referrals page already makes, but it does not change what a determined person could farm. Six throwaway accounts and six trades is still six months of Pro. The control that would actually close that is email confirmation, which is a Supabase setting currently switched off — the code for it is already in place and waiting.

02 Decided · built

A card that declines on day 14 bought 28 days of Pro

Your call, 15 September Grace drops to 7 days, and only for a subscription that has actually paid us at least once. A card that declines at the end of a trial now gets none at all. Built and tested; the database change is on dev and waiting for production.

When a renewal payment fails, we give the customer 14 days of grace before their features switch off. That is deliberate and generous: their card expired, the bank held it, and Stripe is still retrying. They have done nothing wrong.

The trouble is that a failed first charge — the one at the end of a trial — looks identical to Stripe. So someone whose card declines on day 14 gets 14 trial days plus 14 grace days: 28 days of Pro without ever paying us anything, and an email confirming they keep their features. It is repeatable with a fresh account.

A$50 of service per occurrence. The code already holds the right principle — it refuses grace to a customer who has never paid — but the status Stripe uses for a failed trial conversion slips past that check.

Worth knowing what this trades away: a genuine new customer whose bank declines a first charge from an unfamiliar merchant — which is common — now loses access immediately rather than keeping it while they sort it out. The day-11 warning email and the payment-failed email both still go out, so they are told twice, but the features stop the same day.

03 Found & fixed

Changing plan during the trial would have charged people twice

This one was not on the list. It turned up while checking whether the billing portal lets a trialist switch plans (item 08), and it was created by this change rather than found in the old system.

Our checkout always creates a subscription — it has no way to modify one. That was fine when a trial was just a date on an account and buying a plan was the only way to get a subscription at all. It stopped being fine when the trial itself became a subscription.

The sequence: sign up, so a Pro trial is running and due to charge A$50 on day 14. On day 2, click the countdown in the nav and buy Trader at A$30. Nothing cancels the first one. The customer now pays A$80 a month having deliberately chosen one plan — and the one they chose is the cheaper of the two.
Fixed, no decision needed Checkout now refuses to start anything while a trial is running, and the in-app upsell sends a trialist to their billing page instead of offering a purchase. Nobody wants to double-bill, so this did not need asking.

It leaves a gap, and item 08 is why. A trialist now has no route to Trader at all: the in-app one would double-charge and is closed, and the billing portal has plan switching switched off. Until that setting is turned on, the trial is Pro at A$50 or cancel.

Yours to decide

Nothing here is blocked on engineering. Each one changes what we charge, who we email, or what we promise.

04 Decision

What happens when someone is charged and meant to cancel

Today the terms say we do not refund a change of mind, that Australian Consumer Law rights are untouched, and that you should email us and we will work it out. That was written for people who chose to subscribe.

After this change the most common refund request becomes "I forgot to cancel" — from someone who never thought of themselves as a customer. We warn them three days ahead by email, and some will still miss it.

A refused refund that becomes a card chargeback costs the A$50 plus a dispute fee, and chargebacks count against the Stripe account. Refunding is almost always cheaper than winning.
  • Option AKeep it case-by-case and unpublished. Maximum flexibility, but the person reading the terms sees only "no refunds".
  • Option BPublish a short goodwill window — refund the first charge if they ask within, say, 7 days and have not used the paid month.
  • Option CStrict no-refunds beyond the legal minimum.
Recommendation Option B. At this volume the cost is negligible, it converts an angry chargeback into a cancelled subscription, and it is a genuinely good line to have on the pricing page while asking people for a card for the first time. I have deliberately left the terms unchanged pending your call.
05 Decision

The founding-member annual discount becomes unreachable

The beta promotion is 80% off the first year — A$120 instead of A$500 for Pro. It only applies to an annual checkout.

The new trial runs on Pro monthly by design, so nobody arriving through it is on an annual plan. Switching to annual later in the billing portal will not carry the discount. After launch, every new signup comes through the trial — so in practice the founding offer stops being claimable by anyone new.

This is the main promotion on the pricing page. A trialist converts at A$50/month instead of A$120 for the year — better monthly revenue, far worse cash up front and worse retention.
  • Option AAccept it. Quietly retire the founding annual offer as the trial takes over.
  • Option BOffer annual at signup as well, which means putting a plan picker back in front of new users.
  • Option CSend trialists a discounted annual link before day 14 — the day-11 email already goes out, so it is a link, not a new system.
Recommendation Option C. It keeps the single-button signup we just decided on, and it puts the offer in front of people at the exact moment they are deciding whether to keep paying.
06 Decision

Whether to invite the 42 people whose trial already lapsed

Production has 7 real users mid-trial on the old card-free version. They keep it — they were sold a trial that takes no card, so we cannot put them on one that charges. They get a banner inviting them to add a card, and their trial ends quietly either way.

Separately there are 42 accounts whose trial expired and who never responded to anything. They were never charged and never told us whether they wanted to continue.

The cost is one email. These are the warmest non-customers we have: they signed up, used Pro for a fortnight, and were never actually asked for a decision.
  • Option AInclude them in the add-a-card campaign, with copy that is honest about the fact their trial ended some time ago.
  • Option BLeave them. Treat the silence as an answer.
Recommendation Option A, provided the email says plainly that their trial ended, no card was ever taken, and nothing was charged. Anything vaguer reads as a system that has just woken up.
07 Decision

Buying Trader mid-trial silently stops a customer's broker sync

Everyone trials Pro. Trader is still purchasable during the trial from two places in the app. Someone who buys Trader on day 2 keeps their Pro features until day 14 — then drops to what they paid for.

Fifteen features switch off at that moment. The one that matters is automatic broker sync: it is Pro-only, so their MT5 or crypto connection stops overnight and is marked as an error. From their side, the product broke the day after they paid us.

A customer who pays A$30 and loses their broker feed without warning is a cancellation and a support ticket, not a downgrade.
  • Option ARemove the mid-trial Trader route. One plan during the trial, switch afterwards.
  • Option BKeep it, but warn clearly at the point of purchase that broker sync is Pro-only and will stop when the trial ends.
  • Option CMake broker sync available on Trader. A pricing change, not a fix.
Recommendation Option B. Cheapest, and it keeps the cheaper plan buyable. Option A is defensible; Option C is a real pricing conversation and should not be made to solve a warning-message problem.

Three things I checked in Stripe

These live in the Stripe dashboard rather than in our code. I read all three off the Stripe API. One is a launch blocker. A caveat on all of them: the key available locally is the test key, and Stripe keeps these settings separate per mode, so each needs a glance in Live mode to confirm. In every case the setting is at its untouched default, so live is very likely the same.

08 Launch blocker

A trialist cannot switch from Pro to Trader — plan switching is off

The whole decision — everyone trials Pro and picks their real plan later — rests on the billing portal letting them switch. It does not. The portal configuration allows cancelling and updating a card, and nothing else:

subscription_update: false  ·  subscription_cancel: true  ·  payment_method_update: true
Default configuration, unmodified. Stripe ships this off; it has to be turned on and told which plans to offer.

So as things stand, the trial is Pro at A$50 or cancel. A customer who wanted the A$30 plan has nowhere to go — and item 03 closed the in-app route because it charged for both plans at once.

  • Option ATurn on plan switching in the portal, in Live mode, and allow both paid plans. Restores the decision as made.
  • Option BAccept it and say so plainly: the trial is Pro, and Trader is something you move to after it converts.
Recommendation Option A, before launch. It is a dashboard setting rather than a build, and without it two other things on this page have nowhere to happen — item 05's annual offer and the "then picks" half of the plan decision. Worth setting proration to "none" at the same time, so a switch during a trial does not raise a surprise charge.
09 Checked · already safe

The beta coupon would expire on the wrong invoice — but cannot reach one

A subscription that starts in a trial raises a A$0 invoice on day one. A coupon set to apply "once" can be consumed by that zero-dollar invoice, so the real charge on day 14 bills at full price after the customer was shown 80% off.

The coupon is duration: once, 76% off. So the hazard was real.
No action needed No code path now puts a discount and a trial on the same subscription — the trial is priced by the server and never carries a coupon, and a discounted annual purchase has no trial. Nothing to change, but worth knowing the coupon is the kind that would bite if the two were ever brought together.
10 Two-step fix

Stripe's own terms checkbox is switched off, and cannot be switched on yet

Stripe can require customers to tick acceptance of our terms on its checkout page and record it against the payment — a stronger record than anything we can store ourselves, because the acceptance and the transaction are the same object.

terms_of_service_url: NOT SET in the Stripe business profile, and the switch that turns the checkbox on is off in our own configuration. Both halves are needed, in that order — Stripe rejects the request if the URL is missing, so flipping our switch alone would break every checkout.

It has been a nice-to-have while checkout was rare. It is about to sit in front of every single signup, which is exactly when it stops being optional.

Recommendation Set the terms URL in the Stripe dashboard first, confirm the checkbox appears, then turn our switch on. Two small steps, and the order matters.
